About Polacanthus

Polacanthus foxii was a herbivorous thyreophoran that lived during the Early Cretaceous, roughly 125 million years ago. Its fossils have been found in Europe. Scientists estimate it was about 5 metres long and weighed around 2,000 kg — roughly the weight of 1.3 cars. In the Early Cretaceous, the first flowering plants appeared and the continents kept drifting apart. As a plant-eater, it fed on the ferns, conifers and other vegetation of its time. Among the 155 dinosaurs in Dinodle, it ranks #100 by length and #65 by weight. It was discovered by Richard Owen in 1865, 161 years ago.
